What is Glioma?

Glioma is a type of tumor that occurs in the brain and spinal cord. Gliomas begin in the glial cells — supportive cells that surround nerve cells and help them function. These tumors can be malignant and arise from glial tissue in the central nervous system.

Common Characteristics

  • Originates from glial cells (astrocytes, oligodendrocytes, ependymal cells)
  • Can be low-grade (slow-growing) or high-grade (fast-growing)
  • Common symptoms include headaches, seizures, and neurological deficits
  • Typically requires medical imaging like MRI for accurate diagnosis

What is Meningioma?

Meningioma is a type of tumor that forms in the meninges, the membrane layers that cover the brain and spinal cord. Most meningiomas are benign (non-cancerous), though some can be malignant. They typically grow slowly and may not cause symptoms for years.

Key Features

  • Most commonly benign brain tumor, accounting for about 30% of primary brain tumors
  • Usually grows slowly and may not cause symptoms immediately
  • Commonly affects middle-aged to older adults, with higher incidence in women
  • Diagnosis typically involves MRI or CT scans showing well-defined masses

What is Pituitary Tumor?

Pituitary tumors are abnormal growths in the pituitary gland, a pea-sized organ at the base of the brain that controls many body functions through hormone production. Most pituitary tumors are benign and do not spread beyond the pituitary gland.

Important Information

  • Most pituitary tumors are benign (non-cancerous) and slow-growing
  • Can cause symptoms related to hormone imbalance or pressure on surrounding tissues
  • Treatment may involve surgery, medication, or radiation therapy depending on size and type
