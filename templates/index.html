Diagnostic Overview and Histopathological Characteristics
{% if tumor_type == 'glioma' %}

Gliomas are primary central nervous system neoplasms originating from glial cells (astrocytes, oligodendrocytes, or ependymal cells). They are characterized by diffuse infiltrative growth patterns into surrounding brain parenchyma, making complete surgical resection challenging.

~30%
All Brain Tumors
~80%
Malignant Tumors
WHO I-IV
Histological Grade
  • Common presentations: Persistent localized headaches, focal seizures, cognitive slowing.
  • Radiological hallmark: Inhomogeneous contrast enhancement with surrounding vasogenic edema.
  • Clinical management: Maximal safe neurosurgical resection followed by concurrent chemoradiation.
{% elif tumor_type == 'meningioma' %}

Meningiomas arise from arachnoid cap cells of the leptomeninges surrounding the brain and spinal cord. The overwhelming majority (>85–90%) are slow-growing, benign WHO Grade I extra-axial tumors that compress rather than infiltrate brain parenchyma.

>85%
Benign (WHO Grade I)
~37%
Adult Primary Tumors
2.3 : 1

  • Radiological hallmark: Dural tail sign with sharp extra-axial margin on contrast MRI.
  • Common symptoms: Progressive focal neurological deficits, visual field changes, cranial nerve palsies.
  • Clinical management: Active neuro-monitoring for incidental cases; stereotactic radiosurgery or resection for symptomatic masses.
{% elif tumor_type == 'pituitary' %}

Pituitary adenomas/neuroendocrine tumors (PitNETs) are neoplasms situated within the sella turcica originating from adenohypophyseal cells. They can be secretory (functioning) or non-secretory, producing clinical symptoms via endocrine hypersecretion or optical chiasm compression.
