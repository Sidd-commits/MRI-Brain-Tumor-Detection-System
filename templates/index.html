Glioma Clinical Profile

Diagnostic Overview & Histopathological Characteristics
Clinical Reference

Gliomas are primary central nervous system neoplasms originating from glial precursor cells (astrocytes, oligodendrocytes, or ependymocytes). Characterized by diffuse infiltrative growth along white matter tracts into surrounding parenchyma, making complete surgical resection challenging.

~30%
All Brain Tumors
~80%
Malignant Tumors
WHO I–IV
Histological Grade
Common presentations: Persistent localized morning headaches, focal/secondary generalized seizures, cognitive slowing, motor deficits.
Radiological hallmark: Inhomogeneous contrast enhancement on T1+Gd with marked surrounding T2/FLAIR vasogenic edema and necrotic core.
Clinical management: Maximal safe neurosurgical cytoreduction followed by concurrent temozolomide chemoradiation (Stupp protocol).

Meningiomas arise from arachnoid cap cells of the leptomeninges. Predominantly slow-growing, benign extra-axial neoplasms that compress adjacent brain parenchyma rather than infiltrating it, frequently attached to the dura mater along sagittal sinuses and skull base.

Common presentations: Progressive insidious focal neurological deficits, visual field changes, chronic headaches, cranial neuropathies.
Radiological hallmark: Sharp extra-axial boundary with the characteristic "dural tail" sign on post-contrast T1 MRI and broad dural base attachment.
Clinical management: Conservative active neuro-monitoring for asymptomatic lesions; complete Simpson Grade I surgical resection or radiosurgery.

Pituitary neuroendocrine tumors (PitNETs / Adenomas) originate in the anterior pituitary gland within the sella turcica. Classified as microadenomas (<10 mm) or macroadenomas (≥10 mm), causing pathology via endocrine hypersecretion or optical chiasm compression.

Radiological hallmark: Sella turcica enlargement with suprasellar "snowman" extension toward the optic chiasm and cavernous sinus invasion.
Clinical management: Endoscopic endonasal transsphenoidal surgical resection; primary medical therapy (cabergoline) for prolactinomas.
